Last week's most significant zero-day activity centered on Citrix. On 30 June, Citrix published an emergency bulletin covering six flaws in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway, headlined by **CVE-2026-8451** (dubbed "CitrixBleed 3"), a pre-authentication memory-overread rated CVSS 8.8 that lets an unauthenticated attacker pull fragments of appliance memory out of an HTTP response. Sceptive-aligned honeypot networks and peer sensors registered exploitation attempts inside 24 hours of the patch, beginning with an actor operating from a Frankfurt IP (146.70.139.154) that swept sensors over roughly a five-hour window and delivered payloads only to hosts returning HTTP 200 to filter for genuinely SAML-enabled, vulnerable appliances, followed by a second operator on 2 July. In parallel, attackers continued exploiting **CVE-2026-0625**, a command-injection zero-day in end-of-life D-Link DSL gateways (dnscfg.cgi endpoint) to run arbitrary shell commands, and **CVE-2026-2441**, an actively exploited Chromium memory-corruption flaw enabling remote code execution in the browser process with no authentication.

On the AI and mass-exploitation front, our sensors captured the same pattern reported across the industry: automated, machine-speed reconnaissance rather than manual probing. Threat actors ran mass scanning of exposed LLM infrastructure, systematically enumerating 73-plus model endpoints (OpenAI-compatible, Gemini, Claude, Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen and Grok formats) against publicly reachable Ollama and MCP hosts, with validator infrastructure tied to the "Operation Bizarre Bazaar" LLMjacking scheme testing exposed endpoints within 2 to 8 hours of public scan results and reselling access at 40 to 60 percent discounts. This mirrors the AI-augmented tradecraft seen in the CyberStrikeAI FortiGate campaign, where low-skill operators used DeepSeek and Claude's coding agent to automate credential brute-forcing and configuration extraction against exposed management ports. Overall scanning volume set records, with individual honeypots sustaining 20 GB-plus of logs per day and peaks near 58 GB in a single 24-hour period. The clearest mass-exploitation signal was Ivanti Sentry: CISA flagged **CVE-2026-10520**, a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated OS command-injection allowing root-level RCE, as exploited, with the observed activity landing on honeypots.

For executives, the products carrying confirmed critical or actively-exploited vulnerabilities last week were **Citrix NetScaler ADC / Gateway** (CVE-2026-8451, pre-auth memory disclosure, exploited within 24h; internet-facing gateways should be treated as compromise-suspect and session tokens rotated), **Ivanti Sentry** (CVE-2026-10520, CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated root RCE, exploitation flagged by CISA), **end-of-life D-Link DSL gateways** (CVE-2026-0625, actively exploited with no vendor fix available given EoL status, so affected units warrant removal from service), and **Google Chrome / Chromium** (CVE-2026-2441, actively exploited RCE requiring immediate browser updates across the fleet). The broader caution is exposure of management planes and AI infrastructure: any NetScaler, Sentry, or LLM endpoint reachable from the internet is being scanned and weaponized in hours, not days, so exposure inventory and out-of-band management access are the practical points of leverage this week.
