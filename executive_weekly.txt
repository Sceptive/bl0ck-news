Two fresh zero-days dominated the week. **SonicWall** confirmed on 2 September that **CVE-2026-83548**, a pre-authentication SSRF in the SMA1000 Appliance Work Place interface rated CVSS 10.0, was under active attack alongside the command-injection flaw **CVE-2026-83549**, affecting the 6210, 7210 and 8200v models. CISA added both to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 3 September. On 4 September **Google** shipped Chrome 152.0.7977.82/.83 to close **CVE-2026-85046**, a V8 type-confusion bug with a confirmed in-the-wild exploit and the sixth Chrome zero-day of 2026.

Exploitation velocity, not novelty, was the week's real story. **JFrog Artifactory** **CVE-2026-82329** (CVSS 9.8, unauthenticated admin-token minting) went from disclosure to live attacks in four days; watchTowr observed operators minting tokens and enumerating users, groups and credential sets, and on 1 September that attribution to human or agentic operators remains unresolved after OpenAI's July agent incidents against the same product. **PaperCut NG/MF** (**CVE-2026-81578**, **CVE-2026-82078**) entered KEV on 31 August following zero-day activity from 26 August. Opportunistic scanning continued against **Langflow** **CVE-2026-0768**, where attackers harvested AWS and OpenAI credentials, and against **Zimbra** **CVE-2026-73570**, with 274 or more exposed servers hit. On npm, the "Trinitite" wave published multiple malicious versions of `@7nohe/openapi-react-query-codegen` inside twenty minutes.

Critical vulnerabilities this week affected **SonicWall SMA1000**, **Google Chrome**, **JFrog Artifactory**, **PaperCut NG/MF**, **Langflow**, **Zimbra** and **Next.js** (**CVE-2026-75604**). Managers should treat SMA1000 appliances as potentially compromised rather than merely unpatched, since MFA seed theft survives patching, and should assume any internet-facing build or AI-orchestration platform will face working exploits within four days of disclosure.
